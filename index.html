Research

Program 01
Alaskome — Genomics & Ethics in Alaska

The Alaskome project represents a deep, long-term collaboration with Indigenous communities in Arctic Alaska to understand the genetic landscape of Alaska Native peoples and develop genomic reference panels that reflect their diversity. Our work is anchored in a commitment to community sovereignty, reciprocity, and the ethical principles of Indigenous data governance. We use whole genome sequencing (WGS), Hi-C, and trio sequencing to build a comprehensive Arctic reference panel — while simultaneously working with communities to establish frameworks that ensure genomic data benefits the people from whom it originates.

Key questions include: How do we integrate Indigenous ethics frameworks with standard bioethics protocols? How can a community-specific genomic reference improve diagnostic accuracy? What does equitable return of genomic information look like in remote, resource-limited settings?

WGS Hi-C Trio Sequencing Genomic Ethics Indigenous Data Sovereignty Reference Panel Development
Program 02
Chromatin Architecture in Development & Aging

The three-dimensional organization of the genome — how DNA folds and loops inside the nucleus — is a fundamental layer of gene regulation that we are only beginning to understand. Our lab applies and extends Hi-C technology to map chromatin architecture across cell types, developmental stages, and in the context of aging. We study how topologically associating domains (TADs), compartments, and enhancer–gene loops change as cells differentiate, and what goes wrong when this organization breaks down in disease.

We leverage computational tools including Juicer/Juicebox, the cooler ecosystem, and custom analysis pipelines, as well as collaborations with clinical partners to connect epigenomic signatures to patient outcomes.

Hi-C Chromatin Folding TADs & Loops Epigenomics Development Aging ENCODE Pipelines
Program 03
Molecular Diagnostics

Our lab developed POLAR (Pathogen-Oriented Low-Cost Assembly & Re-Sequencing), a whole-genome sequencing-based diagnostic method for SARS-CoV-2 and other pathogens that achieves clinical-grade sensitivity and specificity at a fraction of the cost of conventional platforms. Beyond diagnosis, POLAR simultaneously assembles the pathogen genome — enabling surveillance and variant tracking from a single test. We continue to develop and validate genomic diagnostic tools for deployment in both high-resource and resource-constrained clinical settings, with an emphasis on pediatric populations.

POLAR WGS Diagnostics SARS-CoV-2 Genomic Surveillance Clinical Translation
Program 04
Microbiology & Infectious Disease

Bridging fundamental microbiology with genomic and computational approaches, our infectious disease work spans the microbiology of emerging pathogens, genomic epidemiology, and translational clinical research. We are situated at UTMB, a premier institution for infectious disease research, and benefit from a rich environment of clinical collaborators and biosafety infrastructure. Projects in this area examine pathogen genomics, host-pathogen interaction, and the use of genome sequencing to improve outbreak response and patient care in infectious disease settings.

People

Principal Investigator
Aviva Presser Aiden, MD PhD
Principal Investigator
University of Texas Medical Branch (UTMB)

Dr. Aviva Presser Aiden is a physician-scientist whose work spans genomics, epigenomics, molecular diagnostics, and the ethics of genomic research in under-represented communities. She leads projects on chromatin architecture regulation in development and aging, community-engaged genomics in Alaska (the Alaskome project), clinical molecular diagnostics, and microbiology and infectious disease. Dr. Aiden is based at the University of Texas Medical Branch (UTMB) in Galveston, Texas.

Research Opportunities

We welcome researchers at all career stages who are passionate about genomics, epigenomics, molecular diagnostics, infectious disease, and the ethical dimensions of genomic medicine. If you are excited about working at the intersection of computation, experiment, and clinical translation — please reach out.

Postdoctoral Fellows

Recruiting

We are interested in postdoctoral candidates with strong backgrounds in computational biology, genomics, epigenomics, or infectious disease. Candidates with wet lab expertise in Hi-C, sequencing-based assays, or molecular diagnostics are especially encouraged to apply.

To apply, please send a CV, a brief statement of research interests (1–2 paragraphs), and contact information for two references to apaiden@utmb.edu with the subject line "Postdoc Application."

Open Position
Postdoctoral Fellow — 3D Genomics of Developmental Regulation

We are seeking a postdoctoral fellow to investigate how three-dimensional chromatin architecture shapes gene regulation during development. The ideal candidate has a strong background in genomics or epigenomics, and experience with mouse models is a plus. You will have the opportunity to apply and extend cutting-edge Hi-C and related methods to developmental systems, with access to state-of-the-art computational and experimental resources.

Interested? Please email your CV to apaiden@utmb.edu with the subject line "Postdoc — 3D Genomics."

Open Position
Graduate Student — Longhorn Genetics

We have an opening for a graduate student interested in the genetics of Texas Longhorn cattle. This project sits at the intersection of population genetics, genomics, and breed-specific biology — with opportunities to contribute to both fundamental science and applied questions in animal genetics. Students with curiosity about large animal or agricultural genomics, evolutionary genetics, or comparative genomics are especially encouraged to reach out.

Interested? Please email your CV to apaiden@utmb.edu with the subject line "Graduate Student — Longhorn Genetics."

Research Associates

We occasionally have openings for full-time research associates with bachelor's or master's degrees in biology, biochemistry, bioinformatics, or a related field. Research associates play a critical role in driving projects forward and are supported in pursuing their long-term career goals.

To inquire, send a CV and brief cover letter to apaiden@utmb.edu with the subject line "Research Associate Inquiry."
